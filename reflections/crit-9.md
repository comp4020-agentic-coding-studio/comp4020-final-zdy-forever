# Crit 9 — All at once

**The breakthrough.** I found the voting problem myself by opening two pages. One stayed connected but received no input for roughly five minutes. When the other requested Skip, the vote could not pass with only one participant responding. I asked the agent to account for people temporarily occupied away from their computers. A retry appeared normal, so I initially believed the correction worked.

That belief needed revision. The earlier presence change ([`d711fc6`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-zdy-forever/commit/d711fc6)) addressed membership changes; an idle but connected person is a different case. In an agent-assisted follow-up on 11 October, the second browser stayed connected without input for about 5 minutes 10 seconds, and Skip still failed after 15 seconds. The [captured events](../docs/evidence/2026-10-11/README.md) make that distinction observable.

I then approved treating nonresponses as abstentions at the deadline, without disconnecting the person. [ADR 0012](../docs/adr/0012-abstention-does-not-block-votes.md) records the cost: one YES can pass if nobody votes NO. The correction later passed local tests and a deployed two-connection check after 310 seconds of inactivity.

**What this changed.** I still see myself as directing agents rather than inspecting every function. However, a successful retry and an agent's explanation are insufficient if the test changes the situation that caused the failure. I need to specify the observable condition: the other person remains connected throughout the vote. The agent can implement and automate that check. My responsibility is to ensure it tests the rule I meant. This was a self-discovered problem, not feedback from a crit discussion; the October follow-up is later evidence, not part of the original presentation.
